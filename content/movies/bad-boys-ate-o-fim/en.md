Captain Howard, mentor to Mike Lowrey (Will Smith) and Marcus Burnett (Martin Lawrence), is already dead, but now he stands accused of working for the Miami cartels. Convinced it's a frame-up, the two detectives decide to investigate on their own and end up on the wrong side of the law, hunted by the police and the criminals at the same time.

## Unleashed camera

Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah, who had already revitalized the series with Bad Boys for Life, go even further here. The camera flies on drones, spins along with the guns and sometimes takes the point of view of the shooter. The result resembles a first-person shooter and delivers some of the franchise's most inventive action scenes, especially in the final showdown.

## The usual duo

The engine is still the chemistry between Smith and Lawrence. After a health scare, Marcus starts to think he's invincible, and that idea yields the film's best jokes. Mike, for his part, struggles with panic attacks, a touch of fragility that Smith plays well.

## Is it worth watching?

The conspiracy plot is predictable and the film has sentimental scenes it didn't need. But as noisy entertainment, Ride or Die delivers what it promises and shows that the duo still has gas in the tank. For fans of the earlier films, it's a safe bet.
