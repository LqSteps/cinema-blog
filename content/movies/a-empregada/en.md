Millie (Sydney Sweeney) desperately needs a job and a roof over her head. When she is hired to live and work in the mansion of Nina (Amanda Seyfried) and Andrew Winchester (Brandon Sklenar), she seems to have hit the jackpot: a room of her own, a good salary and a wealthy family straight out of a magazine ad. Except the lady of the house changes mood without warning, the couple's daughter treats her with contempt, and the door to the little attic room locks from the outside.

## The pleasure of suspicion

Freida McFadden's book became a phenomenon precisely because it plays with what the reader thinks they know, and Paul Feig's film keeps that mischief intact. For much of the story, the question is simple: who is lying in this house? Feig, best known for comedies, keeps a sour sense of humor simmering beneath the tension.

## Two actresses in a duel

Seyfried has a blast with a character who could be victim or villain from one scene to the next, and Sweeney anchors the film with a protagonist who hides more than she shows. The chemistry between the two is what makes the suspense work even when the script strains credibility.

## Is it worth watching?

The Housemaid doesn't reinvent the domestic thriller, but it is exactly the kind of movie you want to see in a packed theater, hearing the audience react. Those who read the book will enjoy comparing. Those who haven't should dodge spoilers at all costs.
