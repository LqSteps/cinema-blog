A card-trick specialist, a mentalist, an escape artist and a street magician receive mysterious invitations to work together. A year later, now known as the Four Horsemen, they put on a show in Las Vegas in which they apparently rob a bank in Paris live and make the money rain down on the audience. FBI agent Dylan Rhodes (Mark Ruffalo) and Interpol investigator Alma Dray (Mélanie Laurent) must work out how they did it and, harder still, prove it.

## A cast in sync

Jesse Eisenberg, Woody Harrelson, Isla Fisher and Dave Franco make up a charismatic group, each with his or her own specialty and brand of arrogance. Michael Caine and Morgan Freeman round out the team as the millionaire who bankrolls the shows and the magic debunker chasing the group. That is too many people for too little screen time, but everyone is having fun.

## A camera that never stops

Louis Leterrier shoots everything in constant motion, with the camera circling the actors and effects that make the magic bigger than a stage could allow. It gives the film a roller-coaster pace, even if it takes away some of the fun of guessing the trick.

## Is it worth it?

The script demands goodwill, and the final twist divides opinion. But Now You See Me is efficient entertainment that grips from start to finish and makes you want to watch again to catch the clues. It spawned two sequels.
