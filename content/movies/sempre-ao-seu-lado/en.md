One night, music professor Parker Wilson (Richard Gere) finds a lost Akita puppy at the train station of a small American town. His wife, Cate (Joan Allen), does not want a dog in the house, but Hachi ends up staying. In time, the dog builds a ritual: he walks his owner to the station in the morning and returns every evening to wait for him at the train exit.

## A true legend

The film adapts the story of Hachikō, the Akita who, in the 1920s and 1930s, waited for his owner at Shibuya station in Tokyo for nearly ten years. Today there is a statue of him there, one of the most famous meeting spots in Japan. Lasse Hallström moves the story to the United States but keeps the essential: loyalty as something silent and unconditional.

## From the dog's point of view

Hallström shoots calmly and, now and then, adopts Hachi's gaze, in nearly colorless images at ground level. Gere, who also produced the film, has a rare naturalness opposite the three Akitas who played the dog. And Jan A. P. Kaczmarek's piano melody grows in importance to the very end.

## Is it worth it?

Hachi is short, predictable and honest. It does not try to surprise, and it does not need to: it is a film about waiting. Keep the tissues close, because almost nobody makes it to the end without crying.
