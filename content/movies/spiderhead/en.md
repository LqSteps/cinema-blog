On a remote island, the Spiderhead prison looks like a resort: the cells have no locks, the food is good and the inmates move around freely. In exchange, they agree to serve as guinea pigs for Steve Abnesti (Chris Hemsworth), a charismatic scientist who tests drugs capable of producing love, fear, laughter or obedience on demand. Jeff (Miles Teller), imprisoned over an accident that haunts him, begins to suspect the experiments when he is forced to choose who will receive a dangerous dose.

## Hemsworth against type

The best thing about the film is seeing Hemsworth away from Thor's hammer. His Abnesti is smiling, vain and frightening, a startup boss who talks about science the way you'd sell a product. He seems to be having a great time in the role, and that's what gives the scenes their energy.

## Big ideas, small script

Based on a short story by George Saunders, the film raises interesting questions about free will, guilt and consent. Director Joseph Kosinski, who released Top Gun: Maverick the same year, creates a clean, elegant look. The problem is that the script abandons the story's sharp satire and ends up as a generic action thriller, with a rushed finale.

## Is it worth it?

Spiderhead has a good cast, including Jurnee Smollett as the inmate Jeff tries to protect, and a premise that would make a great Black Mirror episode. As a movie, though, it leaves a sense of missed opportunity. Available on Netflix, it works for a night with no big expectations.
