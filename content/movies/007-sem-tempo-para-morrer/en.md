Bond (Daniel Craig) has left the secret service and is trying to live in peace in Jamaica. The calm lasts until his friend Felix Leiter (Jeffrey Wright), of the CIA, asks for help rescuing a kidnapped scientist. The mission puts him in the path of Safin (Rami Malek), owner of a bioweapon that can target people by their DNA, and forces him to face a past that involves Madeleine Swann (Léa Seydoux).

## The end of an era

Since Casino Royale, Craig's films have told one continuous story, and this is the first Bond conceived as a conclusion. Director Cary Joji Fukunaga treats every loose end with seriousness. The opening sequence, in Italy, is among the best of the Craig era, and the script, with a contribution from Phoebe Waller-Bridge, finds room for humor.

## A cast that steals scenes

Ana de Armas appears for only a few minutes as Paloma, a rookie agent in Cuba, and she is one of the best things in the film. Lashana Lynch, as the new 007, creates a fun rivalry with Bond. The weak point is Safin: Malek has presence, but the villain has vague motives and little time to frighten.

## Is it worth it?

It runs nearly three hours, and the pace sags in the last act. But for anyone who has followed Craig since 2006, the ending carries an emotional weight rare in the franchise. Billie Eilish's theme song won the Oscar. Watch it after the previous four.
