Bob Lee Swagger (Mark Wahlberg) was the best sniper in the U.S. Marine Corps, until a mission in Africa ended with his partner's death and with him abandoned by his superiors. Years later, living in isolation in the mountains with his dog, he is approached by a colonel (Danny Glover) who asks for his help in stopping an assassination attempt on the president. It's a trap, and Swagger becomes the most wanted man in the country.

## Precision as style

Antoine Fuqua, of Training Day, loves technical detail, and the film has fun with it: wind, distance, humidity, even the curvature of the Earth. That obsession lends credibility to scenes that would otherwise be pure exaggeration. Swagger's escape, wounded, through the streets of Philadelphia and the shootout in the snowy mountains are well-choreographed, tangible action.

## A hero of few words

Wahlberg is great as a quiet, distrustful, practical man who solves everything with patience and good aim. Michael Peña, as the rookie FBI agent who starts to doubt the official story, is the perfect counterpoint. The script, based on a novel by Stephen Hunter, has a distrust of institutions that still sounds current.

## Is it worth it?

Shooter is predictable, the villains are cartoonish and the ending embraces revenge without shame. But it's exactly the kind of action movie that works on a Saturday night: direct, well shot and satisfying. In 2016, it inspired a TV series.
