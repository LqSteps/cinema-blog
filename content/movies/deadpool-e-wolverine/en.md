Wade Wilson (Ryan Reynolds) tried to retire as Deadpool and became a used-car salesman. Until the TVA, the agency that polices the multiverse's timelines, shows up with news: his universe is about to vanish. The only way out seems to be finding a Wolverine (Hugh Jackman) willing to help, and the one he finds is considered the worst of them all.

## Meta-humor at maximum dose

If the earlier films already broke the fourth wall, this one demolishes it. Deadpool comments on Disney's purchase of Fox, Marvel's uneven phase and even Jackman's career, since he had bid farewell to the character in Logan. Director Shawn Levy also saves cameos that will draw screams from anyone who grew up on the superhero movies of the 2000s.

## Finally, the yellow suit

The most affectionate side of the film belongs to Jackman. His Logan here is bitter and ashamed of himself, and the script gives him room for more than jokes. Seeing the actor finally in the classic comic-book costume is a gift to fans, and the fights between the two leads are brutal and funny in equal measure.

## Is it worth watching?

The plot is thin and demands solid knowledge of Marvel and the old X-Men. But as a party for fans, Deadpool & Wolverine is irresistible. It's no accident that it became the highest-grossing R-rated film of all time.
