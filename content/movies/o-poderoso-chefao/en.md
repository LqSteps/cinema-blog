The film opens at a wedding. While the party goes on in the garden, Don Vito Corleone (Marlon Brando) sits in a dark study receiving people who come to ask favors on the one day he can't refuse. Within minutes, Coppola lays it all out: the family, the business and the thin line between them.

## Michael's transformation

The heart of the film isn't Vito but his youngest son, Michael (Al Pacino), the war hero who swears he has nothing to do with his father's business. Watching Pacino's face change, scene after scene, is watching a decent man discover what he's capable of.

## Shadows and a sad waltz

Gordon Willis shot much of the film almost in darkness, often hiding Brando's eyes in shadow. Nino Rota's score, as melancholy as a Sicilian folk song, warns from the very start that this story won't end well.

## Is it worth it?

Based on Mario Puzo's novel, The Godfather won the Oscars for Best Picture, Best Actor and Best Adapted Screenplay. It runs nearly three hours, and there isn't a wasted scene. The trailer below is from the 4K restoration released for the film's 50th anniversary.
