As a teenager, stunt motorcyclist Johnny Blaze makes a pact with the demon Mephisto (Peter Fonda) to save his father from illness. The price comes years later: now an adult famous for impossible jumps, Johnny (Nicolas Cage) is forced to become the Ghost Rider, the devil's bounty hunter of souls, a flaming skull on a motorcycle of fire. His first task is to stop Blackheart, Mephisto's rebellious son.

## Cage in his element

Cage is a self-declared fan of the character, and it shows on screen, for better and for worse. His Johnny Blaze is eccentric: he eats jelly beans out of martini glasses and listens to the Carpenters to keep the monster in check. They are strange choices, and they give the film a personality the script alone does not have.

## Fire and a hidden western

The transformations and the burning bike impressed in 2007 and still work on a small screen. The best of the film, though, is its western side, with Sam Elliott as a mysterious cemetery caretaker who knows more than he lets on. Eva Mendes, as the love interest, has little to do, and the villains are generic.

## Is it worth it?

Ghost Rider is uneven, with weak dialogue and a tone that cannot decide between horror and comedy. Even so, it became a guilty pleasure for many, thanks to Cage and the hero's look. It got a sequel in 2012, even more over the top.
