Eddie Brock (Tom Hardy) and Venom, the alien symbiote living inside him, are on the run. Hunted by the authorities, they have also become the target of Knull, the creator of the symbiotes, who is imprisoned on the other side of the universe and sends monsters to capture them. With nowhere safe to go, the pair cross the United States toward New York, while soldiers and scientists led by Rex Strickland (Chiwetel Ejiofor) and Dr. Teddy Paine (Juno Temple) join the hunt.

## A couple's comedy with teeth

What has always worked in these films is the relationship between Eddie and Venom, who bicker like an old married couple, and here it remains the best part. Kelly Marcel, screenwriter of the previous two films and a first-time director, knows this and leans into absurd moments, like a stop in Las Vegas and a dance to ABBA. Hardy, once again, throws himself into it without fear of looking ridiculous.

## Threats without faces

The problem is everything else. Knull appears only briefly and works more as a promise for the future than as a villain, and the monsters chasing the duo are digital creatures with no personality. Ejiofor and Temple get thin characters, and the subplot about the secret military base feels like it came from another movie.

## Is it worth it?

For fans of the first two films, The Last Dance offers more of the same and an ending that tries hard to be moving. For everyone else, it is a weak farewell to a trilogy that never quite knew what it wanted to be. The best moments are between Eddie and Venom.
