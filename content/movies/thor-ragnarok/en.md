Thor (Chris Hemsworth) comes home to a string of problems: his father, Odin, has vanished, and Hela (Cate Blanchett), the goddess of death, appears to claim the throne of Asgard. In a disastrous showdown, Thor loses his hammer and ends up on Sakaar, a junkyard planet ruled by the eccentric Grandmaster (Jeff Goldblum). There, he is forced to fight as a gladiator against the local champion, whom he knows very well: the Hulk.

## A funny Thor

The hero's first two solo films were serious and rather forgettable. New Zealander Taika Waititi throws that solemnity out and bets on Hemsworth's comic talent, which turns out to be enormous. Improvisation drives several scenes, and the director himself appears as Korg, a soft-spoken stone giant who became a fan favorite.

## Album-cover visuals

Inspired by Jack Kirby's comics, the film is an explosion of neon colors, with sets that look straight off a prog-rock album cover. Mark Mothersbaugh's synth-heavy score and Led Zeppelin's "Immigrant Song" in the battles give the film a personality all its own. Tessa Thompson as Valkyrie and Tom Hiddleston as Loki round out a perfectly tuned cast.

## Is it worth it?

The excess of jokes sometimes weakens the dramatic moments, and Hela deserved more screen time. But Thor: Ragnarok is one of Marvel's most fun films and a rare case of a franchise saved by a radical change of tone. It works even for people who don't follow the universe.
