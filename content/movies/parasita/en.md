The Kim family lives in a cramped semi-basement, folding pizza boxes and hunting for a Wi-Fi signal. When the son lands a tutoring job at the home of the wealthy Parks, a plan takes shape: one by one, every Kim will find a way into that house. This review is spoiler-free, and Parasite deserves to be seen knowing nothing more than that.

## The house as a character

The Park mansion was built specifically for the film, and Bong shoots it like a game board. Staircases separate those above from those below, and the director uses that geography constantly: going up and coming down is never just movement here.

## Laughing until you can't

The first act is a delightful comedy, with a cast led by Song Kang-ho at the top of his game. Little by little the laughter turns uncomfortable, and by the time you notice, you're in another genre. The shift in tone is so precise it feels like a magic trick.

## Is it worth it?

Winner of the Palme d'Or at Cannes and the first non-English-language film to win the Oscar for Best Picture, Parasite earns every award. It's funny, tense and sad, sometimes in the same scene. Watch it with someone so you can talk about it afterward.
