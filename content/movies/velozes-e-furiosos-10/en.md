Ten years ago, in Rio de Janeiro, Dom Toretto (Vin Diesel) and his crew robbed the vault of drug lord Hernan Reyes and caused his death. What they didn't know is that Reyes had a son. Now Dante (Jason Momoa) appears, determined to destroy everything Dom loves, and begins by framing the family as terrorists the world over.

## Momoa unleashed

The film's big asset is Momoa. His Dante is theatrical, mocking and unpredictable, with painted nails and flamboyant costumes, and he seems to be in a more fun movie than the rest of the cast. He gives the tenth chapter an energy the series hadn't had in a long time. The return of familiar faces and the arrival of Brie Larson and Alan Ritchson round out a huge cast.

## Everything bigger, everything farther

Louis Leterrier, who took over directing in a hurry with filming already under way, stages chases in Rome, London, Portugal and Antarctica. The Rome sequence, with a giant bomb rolling through the streets, is the peak of absurdity. The plot, however, splits into too many threads, and some characters have very little to do.

## Is it worth it?

Fast X is the beginning of the end of the saga and finishes on a cliffhanger that leaves everything open, which may frustrate. For fans, it's worth it for the villain and the impossible set pieces. For anyone who hasn't followed the series, it's better to start with another chapter.
