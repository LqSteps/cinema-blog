When the film begins, Superman (David Corenswet) has already been in action for three years and, for the first time, has been defeated. His intervention in a conflict between two countries has become a diplomatic crisis, and billionaire Lex Luthor (Nicholas Hoult) seizes the opportunity to convince the world that the hero is a threat. Meanwhile, Lois Lane (Rachel Brosnahan), who knows exactly who he is, questions her boyfriend's choices.

## No origin story, no cynicism

James Gunn skips the story of Krypton, which everyone already knows, and throws the viewer into a world full of metahumans, giant monsters and a super-dog named Krypto. The tone is colorful and optimistic, closer to the comics than to the gloomy films that came before. The director of Guardians of the Galaxy balances the absurd and the emotional well.

## The man behind the cape

Corenswet is convincing both as a hero and as a clumsy journalist, and finds something brave and timely in the character's goodness. His scenes with Brosnahan, especially an interview that turns into a lovers' quarrel, are among the best in the film. Hoult plays a furious, envious Lex, and Nathan Fillion is a delight as Green Lantern Guy Gardner.

## Is it worth it?

Too many characters leave the plot cramped, and not every joke lands. But Superman gives the hero back the hope that is his trademark and is a promising start for the new DC. When John Williams' classic theme kicks in, it's impossible not to smile.
