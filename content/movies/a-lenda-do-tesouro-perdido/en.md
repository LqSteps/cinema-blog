Since childhood, Benjamin Franklin Gates (Nicolas Cage) has heard his family's legend of a treasure hidden by the founders of the United States. As an adult, he discovers that the next clue is in an invisible map on the back of the Declaration of Independence. When his former partner, Ian Howe (Sean Bean), decides to steal the document, Ben reaches an absurd conclusion: to protect it, he has to steal it first.

## Indiana Jones with a magnifying glass

Jon Turteltaub's film, produced by Jerry Bruckheimer, swaps jungles and temples for museums, libraries and old churches. The puzzles involve Freemasonry, hundred-dollar bills and Benjamin Franklin's glasses, and the script takes care to explain every clue. The theft at the National Archives, during a gala, is the high point, a small heist movie inside the movie.

## A likable team

Cage is more restrained than usual and works well as a history nerd with a hero's courage. Justin Bartha, as the sarcastic hacker friend, gets the best lines, and Diane Kruger completes the trio as the archivist dragged into the adventure. Jon Voight plays Ben's skeptical father.

## Is it worth it?

National Treasure has no pretension of being plausible, and that is for the best. It is a well-built family adventure, with just the right amount of pace and humor. It got a sequel in 2007 and, years later, a series on Disney+.
