Before he becomes the suave spy everyone knows, James Bond (Daniel Craig) has just earned his license to kill and still makes plenty of mistakes. His first big mission is to beat Le Chiffre (Mads Mikkelsen), banker to international terrorism, in a high-stakes poker tournament in Montenegro. At his side is Vesper Lynd (Eva Green), the British Treasury agent assigned to watch the money and, while she's at it, Bond himself.

## A Bond who bleeds

After twenty films, the series had turned into a collection of gadgets and puns. Martin Campbell throws all of it out. The foot chase through a construction site in Madagascar, right at the start, sets the new tone: Craig runs, stumbles and crashes through walls instead of going around them. This Bond gets beaten up, feels pain and makes mistakes out of pride.

## Cards on the table

The most surprising thing is that the heart of the film is a card game. Campbell turns poker into a duel of glances, bluffs and a tear of blood on Mikkelsen's face. And the relationship with Vesper, written with razor-sharp dialogue, gives the agent something he has almost never had: a heart at stake.

## Is it worth watching?

Casino Royale reinvented the franchise without losing its charm, carried along by Chris Cornell's "You Know My Name". The final third runs longer than it needs to, but this is the best starting point for anyone who has never seen a 007 film.
