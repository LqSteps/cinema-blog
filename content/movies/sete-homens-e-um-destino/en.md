In 1879, the small town of Rose Creek lives under the thumb of industrialist Bartholomew Bogue (Peter Sarsgaard), who wants the residents' land for his gold mine and does not hesitate to kill anyone who resists. A widow, Emma Cullen (Haley Bennett), seeks out bounty hunter Sam Chisolm (Denzel Washington) to help them. He accepts and begins to gather a group of outlaws, sharpshooters and misfits.

## A remake of a remake

The story was already told in Akira Kurosawa's Seven Samurai and in the 1960 western with Yul Brynner and Steve McQueen. Fuqua makes no attempt to reinvent the formula: the pleasure is in watching the group come together, each member with a skill and a joke. Chris Pratt brings his usual charisma as a gambler, and Ethan Hawke has the heaviest moments as a gunman haunted by war.

## Gunpowder and dust

The film saves its action for the big final battle, a long, well-edited sequence that fills nearly the entire last third. The score was James Horner's last; he died before finishing it, and Simon Franglen completed it, with Elmer Bernstein's classic theme in the credits.

## Is it worth it?

The characters are thinly developed and the villain is one-dimensional. But as an old-fashioned western, with a charismatic cast and well-shot gunfights, The Magnificent Seven entertains. Washington, in a hat and on horseback, seems born for the genre.
