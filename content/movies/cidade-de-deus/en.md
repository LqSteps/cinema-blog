Based on Paulo Lins's novel, City of God follows three decades of the Rio de Janeiro neighborhood through the eyes of Rocket (Alexandre Rodrigues), a boy who wants to be a photographer while crime organizes itself around him. On the opposite side is Li'l Zé (Leandro Firmino), who grows from a dangerous kid into the boss of the drug trade.

## A cast that came from the streets

Much of the cast had never acted before. The young performers were found in Rio's communities and trained in workshops, which gives the film a naturalness no famous actor could match. The lines sound improvised because, often, that's how they were born.

## Form that tells the story

César Charlone's cinematography and Daniel Rezende's editing change with the era: the 1960s have warm light and the rhythm of a chronicle; the 1980s, a nervous camera and cold colors. The film doesn't explain the escalation of violence, it makes you feel it in the very body of the image.

## Is it worth it?

It's a hard film, and some scenes are difficult to watch. But City of God never turns violence into empty spectacle: the gaze of Rocket, watching from behind the camera, is what holds everything together. Nominated for four Oscars, it's one of those films that changes how you see a country's cinema.
