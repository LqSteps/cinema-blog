The Entity, the artificial intelligence that slipped out of control in the previous film, has infiltrated defense systems around the world, and the nuclear powers eye one another with suspicion. Ethan Hunt (Tom Cruise) seems to be the only one who can stop it, but to do so he must find a sunken submarine and convince the very governments hunting him that he is not the enemy.

## A farewell on a grand scale

A direct sequel to Dead Reckoning Part One, Christopher McQuarrie's film ties up loose ends from the entire series and brings back characters and objects fans will recognize. The first hour has too much talk and explanation, but the goal is clear: to give every decision by Hunt and his team the weight of a finale, with Hayley Atwell, Simon Pegg and Ving Rhames.

## Cruise versus gravity

Two sequences justify the ticket. One, underwater, is claustrophobic and almost silent. The other, with Cruise hanging from the wing of a biplane in mid-flight, is the kind of scene action cinema hardly makes anymore. It is impossible not to think about the real risk while you watch.

## Is it worth it?

The Final Reckoning is too long and takes its own mythology too seriously. Even so, it delivers the spectacle the series has promised since 1996 and works as a worthy farewell, if it really is one. See it on the biggest screen you can find.
