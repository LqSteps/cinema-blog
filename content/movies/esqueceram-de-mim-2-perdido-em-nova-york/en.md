A year after being left behind at home, Kevin (Macaulay Culkin) travels with his family to Florida, or almost. In the airport chaos, he boards the wrong plane and lands in New York, with his father's bag and a credit card in his pocket. Staying at a luxury hotel, he lives every child's dream, until he runs into Harry and Marv (Joe Pesci and Daniel Stern), the pair of burglars who have just escaped from prison.

## The same recipe, on a bigger scale

Director Chris Columbus and writer John Hughes know the audience wants to see the first film again, and they deliver exactly that: Kevin alone, a lonely figure who only looks scary, and an elaborate plan of traps. The difference is the setting. A New York decked out for Christmas, from Rockefeller Center to Central Park, becomes an amusement park.

## Traps and heart

The traps are crueler and more cartoonish, and Pesci and Stern take a beating like animated characters. Tim Curry, as the suspicious concierge, makes for great scenes. And Kevin's friendship with the pigeon lady in the park, played by Brenda Fricker, brings the film's most delicate moment.

## Is it worth it?

It is almost a remake of the original. But Home Alone 2 became a year-end tradition on its own merits, with John Williams' Christmas score and a Culkin at the height of his charm. Perfect for December.
