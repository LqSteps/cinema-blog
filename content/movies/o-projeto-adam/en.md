In 2050, pilot Adam Reed (Ryan Reynolds) steals a time-travel-capable ship and tries to return to 2018 to take care of something personal. Something goes wrong, and he lands in 2022, in the backyard of the house where he grew up. There he meets himself at 12 (Walker Scobell): a scrawny, sarcastic boy grieving his father's recent death. Together, they have to fix the future and, while they're at it, the past.

## Two Ryan Reynoldses

The film's best idea is the meeting between the two versions of the character. Scobell mimics Reynolds' mannerisms and quick wit with impressive precision, and the conversations between a bitter adult and the child who doesn't know he's going to become that are the heart of the story.

## Family at the center

Shawn Levy, of Real Steel, reunites with Reynolds after Free Guy and again bets on the actor's charisma. Mark Ruffalo, as the scientist father, and Jennifer Garner, as the mother trying to hold things together, deliver moments of genuine emotion; a conversation in a bar between Garner and her grown-up son is the most beautiful scene.

## Is it worth it?

The time-travel logic doesn't hold up to much scrutiny, and the villain is forgettable. But The Adam Project is a light, funny, sentimental adventure in the spirit of Back to the Future. Available on Netflix, it's a great pick to watch with the kids.
