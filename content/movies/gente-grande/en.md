In 1978, five boys won a school basketball championship together. Three decades later, the death of their old coach brings the group together for the funeral and a Fourth of July weekend at a lake house. Lenny (Adam Sandler) has become a successful Hollywood agent, Eric (Kevin James) and Kurt (Chris Rock) deal with family routine, Marcus (David Spade) is still single and Rob (Rob Schneider) turns up with a much older wife.

## Vacation on film

Grown Ups is built like a travel photo album: the friends tease each other, the kids discover life away from their video games and the adults try to prove they haven't lost their touch. Director Dennis Dugan, a regular Sandler collaborator, doesn't worry much about plot, and the film moves from sketch to sketch.

## The usual humor

Anyone who knows Sandler knows what to expect: crude gags, jokes about appearance and an unashamed sentimentality at the end. Not everything works, and some gags have aged badly. But the feeling that the cast is genuinely having fun carries over to the screen, and the water park scenes and the basketball rematch deliver good laughs.

## Is it worth watching?

It's not a great film, and Sandler has made far better comedies. But Grown Ups became a family-night favorite for a reason: it's light, predictable and comfortable, like a reunion with old friends. In 2013 it got a sequel.
