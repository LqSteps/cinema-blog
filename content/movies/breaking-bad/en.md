Walter White (Bryan Cranston) is a chemistry teacher in Albuquerque: polite, underpaid and invisible. When he's diagnosed with cancer, he does a cold calculation: he needs to leave money for his family. His solution is to cook methamphetamine with the help of a former student, the hapless Jesse Pinkman (Aaron Paul).

## From Mr. Chips to Scarface

Creator Vince Gilligan summed up the idea like this: turn a mild-mannered teacher into a crime boss. The pleasure lies in how slowly it happens, one wrong choice at a time, until the audience realizes it's rooting for someone who no longer deserves it.

## Every episode counts

Across five seasons, the show barely wastes a scene. Details planted in the first episodes come back years later, and the New Mexico desert, shot in saturated colors, becomes a character of its own.

## Is it worth it?

Bryan Cranston won four Emmys for the role, and the show ended at its peak, something rare in television. It was followed by the film El Camino and the excellent Better Call Saul. If you've never seen it, start with the pilot: it already shows everything the series will become.
