Dom (Vin Diesel) and Letty (Michelle Rodriguez) are on their honeymoon in Havana when a mysterious woman, the hacker Cipher (Charlize Theron), shows up with something that forces Dom to work for her. Soon after, he betrays his own crew on a mission in Berlin. To stop him, Hobbs (Dwayne Johnson) and the usual gang team up with an old enemy: Deckard Shaw (Jason Statham).

## The family against Dom

It's the first film in the series without Paul Walker, and the idea of pitting Dom against his friends gives the script a fresh conflict. Charlize Theron plays a cold, calculating villain, and the taunt-filled rivalry between Johnson and Statham is among the best things in the film. Statham, in particular, gets a sequence on a plane, carrying a baby, that is pure action comedy.

## Physics? What for?

Director F. Gary Gray embraces the excess the series adopted from the fifth film on. In New York, hundreds of cars hacked by Cipher plummet from parking garages and flood the streets like a zombie herd. In the climax, in Russia, the crew faces a nuclear submarine on a frozen sea. It's ridiculous, and the film knows it.

## Is it worth it?

The story is silly, the speeches about family repeat themselves and the film is too long. But as spectacle, The Fate of the Furious easily delivers what it promises. Anyone who likes the series' superheroes-on-wheels phase will leave satisfied.
