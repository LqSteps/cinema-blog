Cecilia (Sydney Sweeney) is a young American nun who accepts an invitation to live in an isolated convent in the Italian countryside, where elderly sisters spend their final days. The place is beautiful and silent, but something is off about the other nuns, the rules and the glances. When Cecilia discovers she is pregnant despite being a virgin, the community begins to treat her as a miracle. She isn't so sure.

## Italian-style terror

Director Michael Mohan draws on Italian horror films of the 1970s, with stone corridors, candles, stained glass and cinematography that alternates between beauty and suffocation. The pace is slow at first, building tension through sound and silence, and it speeds up mercilessly in the final act. There are a few cheap scares, but the nightmare atmosphere works.

## Sweeney beyond the looks

Sweeney, who also produced the film and fought for years to get it made, delivers her most physical work here. Cecilia's transformation from a shy, devout girl into someone desperate is convincing. And the final shot, long and uncut, stays with you for a very long time.

## Is it worth it?

Immaculate runs only 89 minutes and doesn't waste time on explanations. It isn't for weak stomachs, and the religious theme may bother some viewers. But for anyone who likes horror with style, it's one of the good surprises of 2024. It pairs well with The First Omen, released the same year.
